## How B2B differs from guest calls

On a guest call you resolve one person's problem and the relationship ends with the call. On a B2B call you are working with someone who will deal with your company again tomorrow. They are busy, they know the vocabulary, and they judge you by whether you save them time. Facts, commitments and follow-through matter more than warmth, although courtesy matters as much.

There are four common situations: calling a **property** to verify a booking or ask for something, answering a **travel agency** about a booking, commission or payment, **escalating** a mismatch with a supplier, and handling a **group** request.

## Before you call

1. **Have the booking reference and the guest's name.** Know the dates, room type, rate plan and what the guest was promised.
2. **Know the policy.** Have the cancellation terms and any special conditions in front of you.
3. **Work out the time.** Check the property's local time before you call, so you do not ring at 3 AM. The [Callback Time Zones](/tools/callback-time-zones/) tool helps.
4. **Decide what you need.** Write the one outcome you want: a confirmation number, an exception, a rate correction. A call with one clear aim is shorter.

## On the call

**Open with who you are, who you represent and why you are calling.** "This is [Name] from [Company], calling on behalf of a guest to verify a reservation. Do you have a minute?"

**Verify with details they hold, not details you read out.** Ask for the confirmation number they have and the dates and room type on their side. If you read yours aloud, it is easy for them to say yes to the wrong booking.

**Read back what you heard.** "To confirm: Mr Smith, arriving the tenth for three nights, one king room, breakfast included." Misunderstandings are cheapest to fix at this moment.

**Agree who does what by when.** Every call should end with a named owner, an action and a time in a stated time zone. "You'll email the corrected rate by 5 PM your time, and I'll update the booking when it arrives."

**Get a name and the time.** Record who confirmed what and when. If the property later disagrees, that is your evidence.

## Agency queries

Agencies usually ask about three things: whether a rate is commissionable, what the commission is, and when it is paid. Use the terms on file, not memory. A **commissionable rate** pays commission to the seller, normally on room revenue excluding tax. A **net rate** has commission already taken out, so the agency sets its own price. Markup and margin are different measures, so always say which you mean. The [Commission and Net Rate Calculator](/tools/commission-net-rate/) works the arithmetic. Quote only what the agreement says, and say "let me check the agreement" instead of estimating.

## Suppliers and service levels

When a partner owes you a response, know the clock. Is it calendar hours or business hours? In whose time zone? When does it start? The [Partner SLA Due-Time Calculator](/tools/sla-due-time/) turns the terms into an exact due time. Put the due time in the note, and follow up as soon as it passes, not a day later.

## Groups

A group request needs numbers early: arrival date, nights, rooms by type, the cutoff date, the complimentary-room ratio and attrition terms. Ask for the rooming list format the hotel expects, and validate the list before you send it. The [Group Block and Rooming List](/tools/group-block/) tool sizes the block and finds errors in a list.

## When things go wrong

- **Overbooking.** If the hotel cannot honour the booking, ask what they will do, in what time frame, and who pays for transport and the difference in rate. Tell the guest only what is confirmed.
- **Rate mismatch.** Compare the rate on your system with the rate the property holds, line by line, and send the evidence. Agree who corrects which side.
- **Payment instructions.** If a property says a virtual card or payment instruction does not work, follow your account's payment process. Do not send card details by email or write them in notes.
- **No answer.** Log each attempt with the time, escalate by the agreed route and tell the guest honestly where it stands.

## After the call

Write the note while it is fresh. Put the property's name and confirmation number, who you spoke with, what was confirmed, what was promised and by when, and what happens next. The [Lodging Case Note Builder](/tools/lodging-case-notes/) prompts for each of these. Then send a short email that repeats the agreement. Written confirmation prevents most later arguments.

## Questions

::: faq
Q: Should I read the guest's full name and booking number aloud?
A: Give what the property needs to find the booking, and no more. Do not share card numbers, and share contact details only if the account's rules allow.
Q: What if the other side is rude?
A: Stay factual and courteous, restate what you need and note the call. Escalate through the account's route if it continues.
Q: How do I handle a language barrier?
A: Slow down, use short sentences, spell names and references with the [phonetic alphabet](/tools/nato-phonetic/) and confirm in writing.
:::
