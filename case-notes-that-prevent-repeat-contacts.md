## Why notes matter more than they seem

Many repeat contacts happen because the last agent's note did not say what was promised, what was checked or what happens next. The customer has to explain again, the new agent has to work it out again, and a small problem becomes a complaint. A good note costs the writer a minute and saves the reader a call.

## What a good note contains

1. **Who and what.** The booking or case reference, the property or partner, and who you spoke with.
2. **Why they contacted.** One sentence in plain words.
3. **What you checked.** The policy, the deadline, the amount, with the source.
4. **What you did.** Actions in order, with times.
5. **What was agreed.** Exactly what you promised, by when, and who owns it.
6. **What happens next.** The next step, the owner and the date.

## A weak note and a better one

**Weak:** "Guest called about cancellation. Explained policy. Guest unhappy. Sent to property."

The next agent does not know which policy, whether the deadline had passed, what the guest wants, what "sent" means or when to expect an answer.

**Better:**

> Cancellation request, booking LB-48213, Sea View Hotel. Spoke with the guest by phone.
> Policy: free cancellation until 8 Nov, 11:59 PM EST (hotel time). Guest called 9 Nov, 2:00 PM Manila time (1:00 AM EST), which is 1 hour 1 minute after the deadline.
> Penalty per rate plan: first night, 150.00 USD.
> Offered to ask the property to review. Did not promise a waiver.
> Emailed the property at 14:10 Manila time. Property to reply by 12 Nov. Owner: me. If no reply by 13 Nov, escalate to the partner team.

The better note is longer by a few lines. Everything a stranger needs is in it.

## Write the time zone every time

A time without a zone is a trap. "Called at 2 PM" could be any of three zones. In lodging work write the property's local time for deadlines and arrivals, and the zone with it. Where a call or a deadline crosses zones, write both. The [Cancellation Deadline Checker](/tools/cancellation-deadline/) and the [Partner SLA Due-Time Calculator](/tools/sla-due-time/) produce times you can paste directly.

## Keep sensitive data out

Notes fields are read by many people and stored for a long time. Do not write full card numbers, card security codes, passwords or full identity numbers. Industry card-security rules do not allow security codes to be kept after a payment is authorised, and they require stored card numbers to be unreadable, so a notes field is the wrong place for either. Record the last four digits of a card if you need to say which card, and follow your account's policy on anything else. The [Call Scratchpad](/tools/call-scratchpad/) and the [Lodging Case Note Builder](/tools/lodging-case-notes/) both check for card-like numbers and mask them before you copy, but the best note never contained them.

## Write what happened, not what you think

"Guest says the room was dirty" is a fact about what was said. "Guest is lying" is an opinion, and it will be read by people who judge the case on it. Put quotes around the guest's own words when they matter, and attribute what the property said to the property.

## Notes for B2B work

A B2B note should be readable by a colleague who has never heard of the partner. Add the partner's name and the contact you dealt with, the reference they gave you, the terms you relied on, and the due time of any promised response, with its time zone. If the call changed a rate or a commitment, say so first.

## A checklist before you save

- Is the reference there?
- Could a stranger tell what was decided and why?
- Are the times written with their zones?
- Did you record who owns the next step and when?
- Is there anything in the note that should not be there?
- Would you be comfortable if the customer read it?

## Questions

::: faq
Q: How long should a note be?
A: As short as it can be while covering the six points above. A few lines is normal.
Q: Should I copy the customer's email into the note?
A: Only if your system has no better place for it, and only what the account allows. Contact details are personal data.
Q: What if I made a mistake in an earlier note?
A: Add a new note that corrects it, with the time. Do not rewrite history unless your system's rules say so.
:::
