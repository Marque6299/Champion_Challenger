## How to use this page

The terms are in alphabetical order. Use the letters to jump to a section. Several measures, such as handle time and utilisation, are defined differently by different teams, so the entry gives the common meaning and tells you what to check. For hotel terms, the contract or rate plan that applies to a booking always overrides a general definition.

## Terms

::: glossary
Abandon rate :: The share of contacts that hang up or leave before an agent answers. Some centres ignore very short abandons, so check the definition.
ACW (after-call work) :: Work an agent does after a contact ends, such as notes, forms and follow-up tasks. Also called wrap-up. It is normally counted in handle time.
Adherence :: How closely an agent's activity matches their schedule, shown as the percentage of time spent in the scheduled state. It measures timing, not quality.
ADR (average daily rate) :: Room revenue divided by room nights sold. It shows the average price paid for each occupied room.
AHT (average handle time) :: Talk time plus hold time plus after-call work, averaged across contacts. Definitions vary, for example in whether transfers count.
ASA (average speed of answer) :: The average time contacts wait before an agent answers.
Attrition :: In group bookings, the share of the room block that may be released without penalty. The percentage is set in the contract.
BAR (best available rate) :: The lowest rate a hotel offers to the public for a room type and date on its standard, usually flexible, terms. Other rates are often set relative to it.
Block :: A set of rooms a hotel holds for a group on specific dates at agreed rates.
Calibration :: A session in which evaluators score the same contacts and compare results, so that quality scores mean the same thing across reviewers.
Callback :: A promise to contact the customer again at an agreed time.
Cancellation deadline :: The last moment a booking can be cancelled free of charge, set in the property's local time. Also called the cutoff.
Channel manager :: Software that lets a hotel update rates and availability on many booking sites at once.
City tax :: A local charge, often per person per night, collected by or for the local authority. Rules and amounts differ by place, and it is sometimes paid at the property. Also called tourism tax.
Commission :: A percentage of the commissionable rate that a hotel pays to the agency or partner that sold the booking.
Commissionable rate :: A rate on which the hotel pays commission to the seller. The base, usually room revenue excluding tax, is set in the agreement.
Comp ratio :: The number of paid rooms in a group block that earns one complimentary room, for example one free room per 25 paid. The ratio comes from the contract.
Cross-highlighting :: In Power BI, selecting a value in one visual to highlight the related data in the other visuals on the page.
CSAT (customer satisfaction) :: The share of survey responses that rate the experience as satisfied, on the scale the survey uses.
Escalation :: Passing a contact to a more specialised or senior team when the first agent cannot resolve it.
FCR (first contact resolution) :: The share of issues solved without the customer needing to contact again. It is measured by survey or by repeat-contact data.
Folio :: The itemised record of a guest's charges and payments during a stay.
Free cancellation :: A rate condition that allows cancellation without a charge until the cancellation deadline.
GDS (global distribution system) :: A reservation network used by travel agencies to reach many airlines, hotels and car companies. Names there are often stored as SURNAME/GIVEN.
Guarantee :: A reservation held with a payment method or deposit so that the room is kept for a late arrival. A non-guaranteed booking may be released at a set hour.
Handle time :: The total time an agent spends on a contact: talk, hold and after-call work.
Hold time :: Time a customer waits on hold while the agent checks something.
Incidentals :: Extra charges during a stay, such as room service or parking, that are not part of the room rate.
Margin :: Profit as a percentage of the selling price. It is not the same as markup: a 25 percent markup is a 20 percent margin.
Markup :: Profit added to cost, expressed as a percentage of cost.
Net rate :: A rate the hotel wants to receive, with commission already removed. The seller sets its own selling price on top of it.
No-show :: A guest who does not arrive and has not cancelled. The booking's no-show terms decide what is charged.
Non-refundable rate :: A rate that cannot be cancelled or changed without losing the amount paid, apart from exceptions the property or seller chooses to make.
NPS (Net Promoter Score) :: The percentage of promoters (scores of 9 or 10 on a 0 to 10 scale) minus the percentage of detractors (0 to 6).
Occupancy (contact centre) :: The share of logged-in time an agent spends handling contacts rather than waiting for them.
Occupancy (hotel) :: Room nights sold divided by room nights available, as a percentage.
OTA (online travel agency) :: A company that sells travel online to the public and is paid by commission or by margin on the price.
Overbooking :: Selling more rooms than are available, which can leave a guest without a room on arrival.
Pay at property :: A rate where the guest pays the hotel on arrival or departure instead of paying in advance.
PMS (property management system) :: A hotel's main system for reservations, check-in, room status and billing.
Prepaid rate :: A rate paid in advance to the seller. Terms on changes and refunds are often stricter.
Property :: The hotel, resort or rental where the guest stays.
QA score :: A score from reviewing a sample of contacts against an evaluation form.
Queue :: The line of contacts waiting for an agent with a particular skill.
Rack rate :: A hotel's standard published price for a room before any discount.
Rate plan :: A named set of price and conditions for a room, such as breakfast included or non-refundable. Cancellation terms belong to the rate plan.
Refresh :: In Power BI, updating a report's data from its source. A report is only as current as its last refresh.
Repeat contact :: A further contact from the same customer about the same issue within a set period.
Resort fee :: A fee charged by some properties per night, often collected at the property, to cover amenities. It may not be included in the displayed room price. Also called a destination fee.
RevPAR (revenue per available room) :: Room revenue divided by room nights available. It equals ADR multiplied by occupancy.
Room night :: One room occupied for one night. Hotels count sales and availability in room nights.
Rooming list :: A list of guest names and room details for a group booking, sent to the hotel before arrival.
Service level :: The share of contacts answered within a target time, for example 80 percent within 20 seconds.
Shrinkage :: The share of paid time when agents are not available to handle contacts, because of breaks, training, meetings or absence.
SLA (service-level agreement) :: A commitment to a response or resolution time between a provider and a customer or partner.
Slicer :: In Power BI, a control on a report page that filters the visuals, such as a date or team selector.
Supplier :: A company that provides the product, such as a hotel or hotel group, as opposed to the agent that sells it.
Talk time :: Time an agent spends speaking with the customer.
Tooltip :: In Power BI, a small box that appears when you hover over a visual and shows exact values.
Utilisation :: Handling time as a share of paid or scheduled time. Definitions vary, so check what the base includes.
VCC (virtual credit card) :: A card number generated to pay a hotel for a booking, with limits on when and how much it can be charged. Follow your account's instructions for handling it.
Walk :: What a hotel does when it is overbooked and sends a guest to another property for the night. Also called walking a guest.
Warm transfer :: A transfer in which the first agent introduces the customer to the next agent and passes on the context before leaving the call.
Wrap-up :: Another name for after-call work.
:::
