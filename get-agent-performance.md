## What an agent performance report is for

Performance data is most useful when it starts a conversation. A report that shows an agent's quality scores and customer outcomes over several weeks helps a team lead decide what to coach, and helps the agent see their own pattern. Used as a league table it does the opposite: it rewards whatever is easiest to measure and teaches people to protect their number. The report above is organised for the first use. Filters let you choose the period and the group, and the visuals show trends alongside single figures.

## The measures you will meet

| Measure | What it tells you | Watch for |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| Quality (QA) score | How a reviewer scored a sample of contacts against a form | Scores depend on the form and the evaluator. Calibration sessions keep evaluators aligned. |
| Customer satisfaction (CSAT) | The share of survey responses that were satisfied | Only some customers answer. Compare response counts, not just percentages. |
| Net Promoter Score | Promoters (9 or 10 on a 0 to 10 scale) minus detractors (0 to 6), as a percentage | Small samples swing it by many points. |
| First contact resolution | Whether the customer's issue was solved without a repeat contact | Definitions vary. It may come from a survey or from repeat-contact data. |
| Escalation rate | The share of contacts passed to another team | Some roles escalate by design. Compare like with like. |
| Compliance and process checks | Whether required steps were followed | A single miss can matter more than the average suggests. |

## Reading it fairly

1. **Look at the sample.** A quality score based on four evaluated contacts is a hint, not a verdict. Prefer periods with enough evaluations to be steady, and say how many there were.
2. **Prefer trends to snapshots.** Three weeks of improvement says more than one high week. A dip after a system change or a new process says something about the change.
3. **Compare with the right group.** Tenure, queue and contact type all shift results. A new starter on complex work will not match a veteran on simple queries.
4. **Read the measures together.** A rising quality score with a falling customer outcome suggests the form measures something customers do not feel.
5. **Check for outliers before drawing conclusions.** One unusual contact can move a small average.

## Using the report for coaching

Start with strengths. Pick one pattern from the trend, not every low score, and bring an example contact to the conversation. Ask the agent what they notice before you say what you see. Agree one change and a date to look again. Data tells you where to look. It cannot tell you why something happened, and the agent usually can.

## Handling individual data

If the report shows named agents, the figures are personal information about colleagues. Share them only with people who need them for coaching, do not paste screenshots into open channels, and follow your employer's data rules. If you believe a score is wrong, raise it with the evaluator or the report owner, who can check the underlying contacts.

## Common misreads

- **Treating a low score as a character verdict.** It describes a sample of contacts, not a person.
- **Chasing the form.** If agents learn exactly what the form rewards, the score rises and the real quality may not.
- **Ignoring response bias in surveys.** The customers who answer are often the happiest or the angriest.
- **Comparing across periods with different evaluators.** A new evaluator can shift the whole distribution.
- **Quoting data without its date.** Check the last refresh inside the report.

## Questions

::: faq
Q: What does GET stand for?
A: The name is part of the report's title and is not defined by this site. Check with the report's owner for what it covers.
Q: Can I export agent data?
A: Only if the report's owner has allowed export. If a visual has no export option, treat it as view-only.
Q: Who do I ask about a figure I think is wrong?
A: The report's owner, or the team that produces the underlying data, such as the quality team for a QA score.
