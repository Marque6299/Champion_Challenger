## What a productivity report is for

A productivity report answers one question: of the time agents were paid for, how much went on work that customers could see, and how much went elsewhere? The answer is not a score of how hard people work. It is a picture of capacity. If handle time is rising, occupancy is falling or shrinkage is growing, the centre can handle fewer contacts with the same people, and planners need to know why. The report above shows these measures for the periods and groups you choose with its filters.

## The measures you will meet

| Measure | What it tells you | Watch for |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| Average handle time (AHT) | Talk time plus hold time plus after-call work, averaged per contact | A fall can mean efficiency or rushed calls. Check repeat contacts and quality alongside it. |
| Occupancy | The share of logged-in time spent handling contacts | Very high values leave no recovery time and can precede burnout. Very low values can mean overstaffing or low volume. |
| Utilisation | Handling time as a share of paid or scheduled time | Definitions differ. Check whether breaks and training are in the base. |
| Adherence | How closely agents follow their schedule | It is about being in the right state at the right time, not about doing a good job. |
| Shrinkage | The share of paid time when agents are not available to take contacts | Breaks, training, meetings and absence all count. Planners add it to their staffing. |
| Contacts handled | Volume of work done | Compare per hour or per shift, so hours worked do not distort it. |

## How the measures interact

Handle time and occupancy move together. If volume is steady and handle time rises, occupancy rises too, because the same contacts take longer. If volume falls and staffing is unchanged, occupancy falls even though each contact takes the same time. This is why a single measure should never be read alone. A team with a short handle time and a very low occupancy is not necessarily efficient. It may simply have too little work for the number of people.

Shrinkage sits between paid time and available time. A centre with 30 percent shrinkage needs roughly 43 people scheduled to have 30 available, because the people not available still need to be paid. The [handle-time guide](/guides/aht-explained/) shows how the pieces of handle time add up, and the [AHT Calculator](/tools/aht-calculator/) lets you work the arithmetic for a call or a shift.

## Reading it step by step

1. Choose the period and the team, and keep them fixed while you compare.
2. Start with volume and handle time. Together they tell you how much work there was and how long each piece took.
3. Read occupancy and utilisation next, to see how much of the available time that work filled.
4. Check adherence and shrinkage to see how much of the paid time was available at all.
5. Compare with the same period last year, or the same weekday last week, so the seasonal pattern does not look like a trend.

## Common misreads

- **Reading adherence as quality.** An agent can follow the schedule exactly and handle contacts poorly, or deviate to help a customer and do well.
- **Ranking agents on handle time alone.** Complex queues, new hires and escalations all lengthen it legitimately.
- **Treating occupancy as a target to push upward.** There is a level beyond which quality and wellbeing suffer.
- **Comparing teams with different mixes.** A team on technical calls and a team on simple queries are not comparable on handle time.
- **Quoting a figure without its date.** Check when the report was last refreshed.

## Questions

::: faq
Q: Why does the report's handle time differ from my own calculation?
A: Teams define handle time differently, for example whether transfers or conference time are included. Check the report's own definition.
Q: Can I see individual agents?
A: That depends on what the report's owner has set up. Treat individual results as confidential and use them for coaching.
Q: How often does the data refresh?
A: That is set by the report's owner. Check the refresh information inside the report before you quote a number.
:::
