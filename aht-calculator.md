## What it does

The calculator has two parts. The first adds talk time, hold time and after-call work in seconds to give the handle time for one call, shown as minutes and seconds. The second takes your total handle time for a shift, in hours and minutes, and the number of calls you handled, and returns the average. Both results are compared with a target that is shared with the AHT Timer.

## How to use it

1. Enter your target in seconds at the top, or leave the saved one.
2. For one call, fill in talk, hold and after-call work in seconds. The result appears as you type.
3. For a shift, enter total handle time as hours and minutes, then the number of calls. Enter 7 hours and 30 minutes as 7 and 30.
4. Read the sentence under each result. It says how many seconds you are under, over or exactly on the target.

## Worked example

**One call.** Talk 300 seconds, hold 60 seconds, after-call work 130 seconds. The total is 490 seconds, which is 08:10. With the default target of 490 seconds the tool says you are exactly on the target.

**A shift.** You handled 54 calls and your total handle time was 7 hours 30 minutes, which is 450 minutes or 27,000 seconds. Divided by 54 calls that is 500 seconds, shown as 08:20. Against a 490-second target you are 10 seconds over.

## The rule behind it

Average handle time is the sum of talk time, hold time and after-call work, divided by the number of calls. Many teams also include conference and transfer time, and some leave out certain wrap-up codes, so the definition on your reports is the one that counts. The calculator uses the plain version so you can see where each second comes from.

The shift average divides total handle time, not total logged-in time, by calls. If you divide your whole shift by your calls you will get a much larger number, because breaks, training and idle time are in there. Handle time measures work on calls, not time at a desk.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Mixing units.** The one-call section takes seconds; the shift section takes hours and minutes. If you enter 8 minutes in a seconds box you will see 8 seconds.
- **Counting calls you did not handle.** Abandoned and transferred-out calls may or may not belong in your calls handled. Match your team's rule.
- **Comparing with the wrong target.** A target for chat is not a target for voice. Set the figure that applies to your queue.
- **Reading one call as a trend.** A single call tells you little. Averages over a shift or a week carry the signal.

## Questions

::: faq
Q: Why does my result differ from my dashboard?
A: Dashboards often count different things as handle time, and they may average a different set of calls. Check the definition on the report, and compare like with like.
Q: Does it save my numbers?
A: Only the target is saved, on this device. The call and shift figures are not stored.
Q: What if I enter nothing?
A: The result boxes show a short prompt telling you which numbers to enter.
:::
