## What it does

You type or paste text and the tool shows each character with its spelling word. Letters use the standard international spelling alphabet, digits use the telephone forms, and common symbols such as the at sign and the dot have their own words. A button copies the whole spelling as text, and a full A to Z chart sits underneath for quick reference.

## How to use it

1. Type the text in the box. It accepts up to 80 characters, which covers almost every name, address and reference.
2. Read the chips from left to right. Each chip shows the character and the word to say.
3. Press **Copy spelling** to paste the spelling into a note or a chat message.
4. Use the chart below when you only need to remember one letter.

## Worked example

You need to give a caller the booking reference LB-4821. Typing it produces this spelling: L - Lima, B - Bravo, - - Dash, 4 - Four, 8 - Eight, 2 - Two, 1 - One. Say the word for each character and pause after the dash so the caller knows the reference has two parts.

For an email address such as maria.santos@example.com, the dot becomes "Dot" and the at sign becomes "At", which are the two places where callers most often write the wrong character.

## The spelling words

The twenty-six letter words are Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee and Zulu. The words were chosen so that they sound different from each other in poor conditions, across accents and over a noisy line. Digits use the telephone forms "Niner" for 9 and "Fife" is sometimes used for 5; this tool shows the plain "Five" and "Niner", which are the forms most callers understand.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Making up words.** "B as in boy" is understood by some callers and mistaken for "D as in dog" by others. The set words are designed to avoid that.
- **Spelling too fast.** Say each word, then pause. Let the caller repeat it back.
- **Forgetting symbols.** Dashes, underscores and dots are where references go wrong. Say them every time.
- **Mixing letters and digits.** Say "Zero" for 0 and "Oscar" for the letter O. The two look alike in many fonts.

## Questions

::: faq
Q: Is the alphabet the same everywhere?
A: The international spelling alphabet is used across aviation, shipping and customer service, which is why callers often know it. Some teams use their own local words. Use whatever your account requires.
Q: Does it send my text anywhere?
A: No. Spelling is done in your browser as you type.
Q: What does "?" mean on a chip?
A: The character has no standard spelling word. Say what the character is in your own words and confirm it.
:::
