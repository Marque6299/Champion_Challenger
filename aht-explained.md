## What it measures

Average handle time, or AHT, is the average time an agent spends on a contact from start to finish. The usual definition adds three things: **talk time**, the time spent speaking with the customer; **hold time**, the time the customer waits while the agent checks something; and **after-call work**, the notes, forms and follow-up that happen once the customer has gone. Some teams also count conference and transfer time, and some leave out certain wrap-up codes. Before you compare your number with anyone's, check which definition is in use.

## How to calculate it

For one contact, add the three parts. A call with 300 seconds of talk, 60 of hold and 130 of after-call work has a handle time of 490 seconds, or 8 minutes 10 seconds. For a period, divide the total handle time by the number of contacts handled. If you handled 54 calls and spent 7 hours 30 minutes, which is 27,000 seconds, on them, your average is 500 seconds, or 8 minutes 20 seconds. Divide by contacts handled, and use handle time, not logged-in time. Logged-in time includes breaks, meetings and waiting, which are not part of the work on a contact. The [AHT Calculator](/tools/aht-calculator/) does both calculations.

## Why teams track it

Handle time drives capacity. If each contact takes longer, each agent can handle fewer in an hour, and the centre needs more people to answer the same volume in the same time. Planners use it, together with volume and a target service level, to work out staffing. That is why it appears on nearly every [productivity report](/reports/productivity/).

## Why it misleads when read alone

A low handle time can mean skill, or it can mean rushed calls that come back. A high one can mean a hard problem, a new hire or a system that was slow. Several things distort the number.

- **Contact mix.** Complex queries take longer than simple ones, so a team with harder work will show a higher average.
- **Repeat contacts.** If an agent ends calls quickly without solving the problem, handle time falls and the customer calls again, so total effort rises.
- **Tools and systems.** Slow screens add hold time and wrap-up that the agent does not control.
- **Learning curves.** New agents take longer. A team that has just hired will show a rise that fades.
- **Averages hide spread.** Two teams with the same average can have very different mixes of short and long calls.

Read AHT beside first contact resolution, quality scores and customer outcomes. When handle time falls and the others hold steady, you are probably seeing real efficiency. When it falls and they slip, you are probably seeing rushed work.

## Where the time goes

| Part | What it contains | What usually moves it |
|------|------------------|-----------------------|
| Talk time | Greeting, verification, explaining and solving | Call complexity, agent knowledge, customer pace |
| Hold time | Waiting while the agent looks something up or asks someone | System speed, knowledge-base quality, access to answers |
| After-call work | Notes, forms, follow-up tasks, tagging | Template quality, tool design, number of systems to update |

Look at the three parts separately before you try to change the total. Teams often find that after-call work is the easiest place to save time, because better templates and fewer systems cut it without touching the conversation.

## Lowering it without lowering quality

1. **Fix the tools first.** A slow knowledge base costs more than any amount of coaching. Fewer clicks to the answer reduces both hold and wrap-up.
2. **Standardise notes.** A good template, such as the ones in the [Call Scratchpad](/tools/call-scratchpad/), makes wrap-up quicker and the notes more useful.
3. **Share the answers that save time.** If one agent has a fast way to handle a common issue, pass it on.
4. **Coach on structure, not speed.** A call that follows a clear order, with verification, diagnosis, solution and recap, finishes sooner because it does not wander.
5. **Measure the whole outcome.** Watch repeat contacts and quality alongside handle time, so a faster call that fails is visible.

## Using a timer

A stopwatch such as the [AHT Timer](/tools/aht-timer/) gives you a feel for the length of your calls, with a target you set. Used as a prompt it helps. Used as a threat it encourages ending calls early. Keep it running through after-call work if that is part of your team's definition.

## Questions

::: faq
Q: What is a good handle time?
A: It depends on the queue, the contact type and the definition. A good target comes from your own history for that queue, not from another team's number.
Q: Is shorter always better?
A: No. A shorter call that does not solve the problem creates a repeat contact. Read AHT with resolution and quality.
Q: Should I include transfers?
A: Follow your team's definition. If transfers are excluded, a call you transfer on may look short.
:::
