## What it does

Hotel partners talk in three measures. **Occupancy** is the share of available room nights that were sold. **ADR**, the average daily rate, is room revenue divided by room nights sold. **RevPAR**, revenue per available room, is room revenue divided by room nights available. The calculator turns three inputs, available room nights, sold room nights and room revenue, into all three, and a second section compares two periods and shows what changed in each.

## How to use it

1. Optionally enter a currency, such as USD.
2. Enter the **rooms in the hotel** and the **days in the period** to fill in the room nights available, or type the available room nights yourself.
3. Enter the **room nights sold** and the **room revenue** for the period.
4. Read the three results. If sold is higher than available, the tool tells you to check the figures.
5. To compare periods, fill in the three figures for Period A and Period B.

## Worked example

A 100-room hotel over 30 days has 3,000 room nights available. It sold 2,100 of them and earned 315,000 in room revenue.

- Occupancy: 2,100 ÷ 3,000 = **70.0%**.
- ADR: 315,000 ÷ 2,100 = **150.00**.
- RevPAR: 315,000 ÷ 3,000 = **105.00**, which is also ADR × occupancy: 150 × 0.70.

**Comparing two periods.** Period A: 3,000 nights available, 1,800 sold, 180,000 revenue. Period B: 3,000 available, 2,100 sold, 231,000 revenue.

- Occupancy rises from 60.0% to 70.0%, an increase of 10.0 points.
- ADR rises from 100.00 to 110.00, up 10.0%.
- RevPAR rises from 60.00 to 77.00, up 28.3%.

RevPAR grew more than either part because the two effects multiply: 1.10 for rate times about 1.167 for occupancy gives about 1.283.

## How it works

The formulas are the ones used across the industry, and they depend on using the same definitions everywhere. Room revenue here means revenue from rooms. If a partner's figure includes breakfast, parking or other income, the comparison will mislead. Available room nights are the rooms in the hotel multiplied by the days in the period. Rooms out of order may or may not be removed from the count, depending on the reporting standard, so ask which one a partner uses. When sold is zero the ADR is shown as not available, and when available is zero all three are.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Comparing ADR across hotels without context.** A high ADR with low occupancy and a lower ADR with high occupancy can give the same RevPAR.
- **Mixing revenue definitions.** Use room revenue only, or total revenue, for every figure.
- **Using different period lengths.** Compare like for like, such as the same month a year apart.
- **Reading RevPAR as profit.** It measures room revenue per room, not what the hotel keeps.
- **Quoting a percentage change in occupancy as points.** From 60% to 70% is 10 points, which is about a 16.7% relative increase.

## Questions

::: faq
Q: Why does the tool say sold is higher than available?
A: Sold room nights cannot exceed available ones in a normal report. Check the period length, the room count and whether the sold figure includes something else.
Q: Does it include taxes?
A: Only if you include them in the revenue. Use the same basis for all periods.
Q: Does it store the figures?
A: No. Nothing is stored or sent.
:::
