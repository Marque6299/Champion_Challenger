## Why this is the call that goes wrong

A guest cancels, the agent says it is free, and the property later charges a night. Or the agent says the deadline has passed when it has not. Most of these mistakes come from one cause: the policy names a time, and the time belongs to the hotel, not to the guest or the agent. Learning to read the wording and convert it takes about a minute and prevents most disputes.

## The main kinds of policy

- **Free cancellation until a cutoff.** The guest can cancel without charge until a stated moment, after which a penalty applies. This is the most common kind.
- **Partly refundable.** Part of the amount is returned. The rate may refund everything except a deposit or a fee.
- **Non-refundable.** The guest loses what they paid if they cancel or change, with exceptions the property or the seller chooses to make.
- **Pay at property.** Nothing is paid in advance, but the booking can still carry a penalty or a no-show charge defined in its terms.
- **Prepaid.** The guest pays the seller in advance. Changes and refunds follow the rate plan and the seller's process.

The cancellation terms belong to the **rate plan** the guest booked, not to the hotel in general. Two guests in the same hotel can have different policies on the same night.

## Reading the wording

Policies describe the cutoff in two ways, and they are not the same.

**Calendar days before arrival, at a set time.** "Free cancellation until 11:59 PM two days before arrival." Count back two calendar days from the arrival date and use the time given. For arrival on Tuesday 10 November, the cutoff is Sunday 8 November at 11:59 PM.

**Hours before the check-in time.** "Free cancellation until 48 hours before check-in." Take the property's check-in time, for example 3:00 PM, and subtract exactly 48 hours. For the same arrival the cutoff is Sunday 8 November at 3:00 PM.

The two wordings give the same date and different times. A guest reading "two days before" may think they have until midnight and be eight hours late.

## The time zone rule

Cutoffs are set in the property's local time. A guest in the Philippines cancelling a hotel in New York has to be inside the deadline as New York sees it. A cutoff of 11:59 PM Eastern on 8 November is 12:59 PM on 9 November in Manila. The [Cancellation Deadline Checker](/tools/cancellation-deadline/) does the conversion for you, including the daylight-saving changes that move the gap between zones. Always say which time zone you mean when you quote a deadline, and say it in the hotel's terms first.

### Daylight saving

Two wordings can behave differently across a clock change. Seventy-two hours before 3:00 PM on Monday 2 November 2026 in New York is 4:00 PM on Friday 30 October, because the clocks went back on 1 November, adding an hour in between. A policy counted in calendar days does not have that effect. If a cutoff falls in the hour that clocks skip or repeat, confirm it with the property rather than guessing.

## Penalties

When the deadline has passed, the policy names a penalty. Common forms are the first night, a set number of nights, a percentage of the total or the full stay. The penalty comes from the rate plan, so do not assume. If the booking includes taxes and fees, check whether the penalty applies to them. The [Booking Price and Change Calculator](/tools/booking-price/) shows the pieces so you can explain what is being charged.

## Changes versus cancellations

Changing dates or room type is often treated as a cancellation and a new booking, so the old policy's deadline and penalty may apply to the change. A change that lowers the price may not return the difference. Read the rate plan before you say a change is free.

## Explaining it to the guest

1. **Say the deadline in their time and the hotel's.** "Free cancellation ends at 11:59 PM Eastern on Sunday, which is 12:59 PM on Monday your time."
2. **Say what happens now.** "That has passed, so the policy charges the first night, 150 dollars."
3. **Say what you can do and what you cannot promise.** "I can ask the property to review it, but I cannot promise the outcome."
4. **Say what happens next.** "I'll contact them today and let you know by email within one working day."
5. **Record it.** Put the policy, the deadline in the hotel's time, the status and what you promised in the note. The [case note builder](/tools/lodging-case-notes/) has a field for each.

## Exceptions

Some properties waive penalties in special circumstances, and some sellers have their own goodwill rules. These are decisions for the property or the seller, not for the agent to promise. Ask, record the answer and tell the guest who decided.

## Questions

::: faq
Q: What if the booking shows the deadline in my time zone?
A: Some booking pages convert for you. If the policy text and the displayed time disagree, trust the policy text and ask the seller to confirm.
Q: What if the guest cannot cancel online?
A: Record the time they tried and any error message. That evidence matters if the deadline is disputed.
Q: Is a no-show the same as a late cancellation?
A: Not necessarily. No-show terms are defined separately and can be stricter.
:::
